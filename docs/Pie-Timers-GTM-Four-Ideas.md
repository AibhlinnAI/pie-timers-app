## 1. 🗣️ Elevator pitch, built on "Aim for the gap"

**Recommendation:** build every pitch on the motto, and always follow it with the line that does the real work: **you can only aim at a gap you can see.** That single move keeps you leading with the problem rather than the condition, and it puts the burden on the tool instead of on the listener.

### How the motto works

The motto carries three meanings, and you get them in this order without ever explaining them.

1. **The cycling meaning.** Look at the gap, not the rock. Stare at the rock and you hit it, because you go where you look. Every rider knows this in their body.
2. **The problem meaning.** You can only aim at a gap you can see. If time is invisible to you, there is no gap to aim at, only the thing coming at you. That is what Pie Timers draws.
3. **The product meaning.** Aim for the gap nobody is serving. Everyone builds timers. Almost nobody builds one that wants nothing from you.

Meaning 1 gets you attention. Meaning 2 is the pitch. Meaning 3 is for builders only.

### One-liners (pick one, say it the same way every time)

- **A. Neutral and clear:** "On a bike you're taught to look at the gap, not the rock. Pie Timers does that with time. It draws the gap."
- **B. Parent-facing:** "It shows the gap between now and done, as a wedge that shrinks. 'Five more minutes' stops being an argument."
- **C. Builder-facing:** "Our motto is aim for the gap. I aimed at the one nobody was serving: a timer with no ads, no tracking and no account."

### About 20 seconds

**Adult version (around 55 words):**

> "On a bike you learn to look at the gap, not the rock, because you go where you look. Time works the same way, except for a lot of us the gap is invisible. Pie Timers draws it. A wedge that shrinks, so you can see what is left. No ads, no tracking."

**Parent version (around 50 words):**

> "Getting my kids off a screen used to cost a meltdown, because five more minutes means nothing if you cannot feel five minutes. Pie Timers shows the gap as a wedge that shrinks. They can watch it close. No ads, no tracking, nobody collecting data about your kid."

### About 45 seconds

> "Anyone who rides is told the same thing. Look at the gap, not the rock. Stare at the rock and you hit it, because you go where you look.
>
> That is our motto. Aim for the gap.
>
> But you can only aim at a gap you can see. A lot of ADHD and autistic people, me included, do not see time. Five minutes drags, an hour vanishes. There is no gap, just the thing coming at you.
>
> So I built Pie Timers. It draws the gap. A wedge that shrinks. You glance, and you know.
>
> Two rules I set myself. No ads. No tracking. Not less, none.
>
> It is on Google Play, under three dollars a month. Tell me if it is wrong for you. I am the whole company."

**Swap-in paragraph for AI and productivity circles:** "The motto has a second meaning, which is the product one. Everyone builds timers. Almost nobody builds one that does not want an account, an SDK and a data trail. That was the gap. Most of the build effort went into what I refused to add."

### Tailoring by community

| Community | Open with | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cycling | The motto, unexplained. It lands on its own. Let them ask what it is for | Turning a ride into a pitch |
| Youth sport coaching and admin | The gap between drills, and kids who can see it closing | Pitching from a committee or coaching role |
| Neurodiversity networks (public ones) | Meaning 2 only. Your own invisible gap, then the app | Speaking for anyone else's experience |
| AI and productivity circles | Meaning 3. The gap in the market and what you refused to build | Claiming AI features you do not have |
| Parents and allied health | The wedge closing, and the argument that no longer happens | Leading with the motto. Go straight to the moment |

### Delivery notes

- **Never let the motto read as "try harder".** Neurodivergent people are told that constantly. Always pair it with "you can only aim at a gap you can see", which makes it the tool's job, not theirs.
- **Do not unpack both meanings in one breath.** If you have to explain the motto, it has stopped working. Say it, then move to the gap you cannot see.
- Say "time blindness" only if the listener says it first. It is a community term, not a clinical diagnosis.
- Never diagnose the person you are talking to. "A lot of people find" beats "you probably struggle with".
- End with a question, not a link. "Does that sound like anything you deal with?" gives them a way out and you a real conversation.
- Do not say: sufferers, deficits, struggles with, helps them cope, fixes, treatment, therapy.
- Do not claim the app treats, manages or improves any condition. See the note on health claims in section 2.
- The ATO Neurodiversity Network is not one of these communities. See section 2.

## 2. ⚖️ Getting Pie Timers in front of neurodivergent people at work

**Status:** conflict of interest declaration lodged and outside employment notification done. That was the right first move and it is now behind you.

**Recommendation:** there is no clean way to recruit ATO Neurodiversity Network members as testers while you hold the Adelaide Site Representative and stand-in Co-chair roles. The only genuinely arms-length route is **public discoverability with zero targeting**: the app is findable by anyone, and you do nothing at all to point the Network at it. Before you approach any colleague in any form, get a written answer from your manager. Do not rely on the existing declaration as permission.

*General guidance only. Not legal or employment advice. Run the final position past your manager, and ask whether the ATO has an ethics or integrity adviser you should also consult.*

### Why the declaration does not unlock this

- Declaring **manages** a conflict. It does not dissolve the power dynamic that creates it.
- Your declaration covered the existence of the business. Recruiting colleagues as testers is a **new and specific activity** that materially changes the risk profile, so it needs a fresh conversation and its own written answer.
- The relevant obligations still sit in the APS Code of Conduct, section 13 of the *Public Service Act 1999*:
  - 13(7): disclose, and take reasonable steps to avoid, any conflict of interest, real **or apparent**.
  - 13(10): do not make improper use of your duties, status, power or authority to gain a benefit for yourself or anyone else.
  - 13(11): behave in a way that upholds the integrity and good reputation of the ATO and the APS.
- 13(10) is the one that bites here. Recruiting testers from a forum you chair is using your status to gain a benefit, whatever your intent.

### The power dynamic, directly

This is the part that does not go away with paperwork.

- **A Co-chair's invitation is not a peer's invitation.** It arrives with the weight of the role attached, whether or not you want it to.
- **Casual makes it worse, not better.** An informal ask over coffee is harder to decline than a formal one, and it leaves no record that you disclosed anything.
- **Members cannot freely decline.** Saying no to the person who chairs the forum you rely on costs something: awkwardness, a sense of having let down the person holding that space. A "no" that costs something is not a free "no", and consent that is not freely given is not consent.
- **Some members want something from you.** Advocacy, support with adjustments, a hearing at the Network. That asymmetry makes a commercial ask land very differently.
- **The Network is a disclosure space.** People bring diagnoses and difficulties there under an implicit safety guarantee. Putting a commercial ask into that space changes what the forum is for everyone, not just the person asked.
- **You would also get worse data.** Critical feedback is hard to give someone with standing over the forum. You would get politeness, not testing. That is a practical argument on top of the ethical one.
- **Play testing adds a privacy ask.** Google closed testing needs each tester's personal Google account email address. Asking a colleague for that, from a position of standing, is a second ask layered on the first.

### What your management plan needs to say

Take these to your manager as a proposal rather than waiting to be told.

- No solicitation of Network members as testers, users or customers, through any channel, including direct message, Teams and in person.
- No use of Network membership information, distribution lists or event attendance for any commercial purpose.
- Recusal from any Network business touching assistive apps, timer tools, vendor selection or workplace adjustment recommendations.
- No use of ATO time, devices, accounts, networks, branding or information for anything AibhlínnAI.
- No use of ATO or Network titles in any AibhlínnAI material. See section 4.
- An agreed form of words for when the topic comes up unprompted.
- A named review trigger list: becoming substantive Co-chair, public launch, any Network activity touching timers or assistive technology, or any approach to you from an ATO area about the product.

### Is there an arms-length route? Tested honestly

| Route | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| **Public discoverability, zero targeting** | **Yes.** The app is on Google Play and the web. A colleague who finds it independently and chooses to use it has made their own decision. Conditions: no hints, no "you should see what I am building", and if a colleague raises it, you do not convert that into a testing ask at work |
| Someone else recruits on your behalf | No. Proxying does not cure the conflict, it conceals it, which is worse |
| An anonymous call for testers via the Network | No. Still your product benefiting from your forum, with your interest hidden |
| The ATO as a customer or pilot site | Different question entirely, and a much bigger one. That is procurement, not testing, and a staff member's own product is a poor fit for it. Park this |
| Public ND communities that happen to include APS staff | Fine. You have no standing there and you are not targeting them as ATO staff |
| Wait until you step down from the role | The clean fix. A short cooling-off period afterwards is still sensible |

### The honest bottom line

The people you are best placed to help are the people you are least free to approach. That is genuinely frustrating and it is worth naming rather than engineering around.

The consolation is practical: **you do not need them.** You need twelve testers for Google Play. Allied health contacts and public ND communities will give you better testers than colleagues would, precisely because their feedback carries no obligation.

### What remains off limits

- Posting, linking or mentioning the product in Network channels, Teams spaces, newsletters, events or mailing lists.
- Using the Network membership list, or any ATO contact list, for outreach.
- Using ATO email, devices, time, systems or branding for anything commercial.
- Accepting a Network endorsement, partnership, testimonial or case study.
- Asking a colleague to post or recruit on your behalf.
- Using your ATO title or Network role in marketing copy, your website, or a store listing.

**The test to hold in your head:** if the reason it would work is that you hold the role, it is off limits.

### Legitimate channels outside your ATO role

**Highest yield first:**

- **Allied health referrers.** Occupational therapists, speech pathologists, psychologists and ADHD coaches recommend visual timers constantly and already have the trust. A short, honest email with a free access code beats any broadcast post. Start here.
- **App store discovery.** Play listing keywords, screenshots and the first two lines of the description. Free, compounding, and the only channel that works while you sleep.
- **Public ND communities.** Reddit (r/ADHD, r/autism, r/AutisticWithADHD), Discord servers, Facebook groups for autistic and ADHD adults. Almost all ban self-promotion. Read the rules, contribute for weeks first, and disclose that you built it every single time.
- **Parent groups.** Facebook groups for parents of ND kids, and school communities. Be careful with your own children's school, where you have a different kind of standing.
- **Australian organisations.** ADHD Australia, Autism SA, Amaze, Reframing Autism, Yellow Ladybugs, I CAN Network and similar. Approach for review or resource listing, not broadcast. Verify each one's current submission process before contacting them.
- **Reviewers and newsletters.** ND-focused YouTube channels, podcasts and newsletters. Offer a free code and no conditions.
- **Your own channels.** aibhlinn.ai, a mailing list, a Discord. Slow, but yours, and nobody can deplatform you.
- **Word of mouth.** Cycling and youth sport contacts, as a person, not as a pitch. Note that if you hold a committee or coaching role in the sports club, the same "do not trade on the role" principle applies there too, at far lower stakes.

### Two other things to check

- **Health claims.** Marketing the app as a productivity or attention tool is fine. Claiming it treats, manages or improves ADHD or autism can push software into therapeutic goods territory in Australia. Keep the copy behavioural and descriptive. Get advice before making any health claim.
- **Consumer law.** Any pledge you publish, including the pricing one in section 4, is a representation to consumers. Make only promises you can keep.

- **Paid at the launch price, locked for life.** Not free, not complimentary.
- **Carole L Ashford is Foundation Member #1.**

**Recommendation:** make **Supabase `identity.product_entitlements` the single source of truth for entitlement on every surface**. Neither store is authoritative, and neither is the Play tester list. Paddle writes into it, Play writes into it, and you write into it by hand for Foundation grants. Freeze the roster before you announce the tier. **Correction to my earlier advice:** I previously recommended signed offline entitlement codes with no server. That was the right answer for an offline app with no accounts. It is the wrong answer now. You already have Supabase identity and an entitlements table that the screensaver depends on. Offline codes would create a second, unreconcilable source of truth and would not be revocable. Use what you have built.

### 🎯 The never-increases promise: you were right, and I was arguing with the wrong thing

I previously split this into two ideas and argued against the never-increases promise. That objection was aimed at the wrong target. I read it as a pledge to your whole customer base, where the exposure genuinely is unbounded. **Scoped to a capped, frozen roster, which is what you actually meant, it is a bounded and countable liability.**

More than that: it is functionally the grandfathering I recommended as the safer alternative, just given a name and a cohort. **Your instinct and my recommendation were the same answer.** Yours is the better version, because "Foundation member #14" is something a person mentions to a friend, and "existing subscriber grandfathering" is a policy nobody has ever mentioned to anyone.

### What the tier is

Up to a hundred people subscribe to Pie Timers at the launch price, and that price never rises for them. Not for inflation, not for cost of living, not for platform fees. It holds on every platform the app runs on.

**They pay. This is not a giveaway.** That distinction matters more than it sounds, and it needs to survive into the public wording, because "Foundation member" invites people to assume free.

This is the position I originally recommended, and you have arrived at it independently. Adopted, and moving on.

Two things it buys you that a free tier would not:

- **Revenue from day one.** A hundred members at A$19 is about A$1,900 a year of actual income, not forgone income.
- **Real signal.** A Foundation member who renews is telling you something true about whether the product is worth paying for. Free members generate expectation without evidence.

### 🔢 Your own accounts do not consume a Foundation subscription

**Direct answer to your question: no, removing mal.oakley@gmail.com does not get you to 100, and it does not need to.** That address was never occupying a slot, because your own accounts should not be in the count at all.

A locked-price subscription sold to the developer is meaningless. You have permanent access because you own the thing, and you would be paying yourself.

**So: exclude, do not reserve.** You do not need to hold slots back. You need three separate lists.

| List | What it is | Count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Foundation roster** | Up to 100 genuine external people. Numbered 1 to 100. Carole is #1 | Capped at 100 |
| **Internal accounts** | malcolm.oakley@aibhlinn.ai, test_account@aibhlinn.ai, any other address that belongs to the business. Full entitlement, `source = 'internal'`, **no member number** | Not counted |
| **Play tester list** | The intake funnel. Contains Foundation candidates, internal accounts, and people who never become members | Not a roster |

**Keep at least one of your own addresses on the Play tester list permanently.** You need it to verify the purchase and entitlement flow after launch, and `test_account@aibhlinn.ai` already does this job. That entry sits outside the 100 and always will.

Giving internal accounts a distinct flag rather than a Foundation grant is what keeps the promise clean. The roster stays 100 real people, the numbering stays honest, and nothing that belongs to the business is ever counted as a member.

- Carole is #1 by your decision. Assign the rest by date of first opt-in, which is defensible and needs no judgement calls.

### What it includes

- **A permanently locked price**, on any platform Pie Timers runs on.
- Access to the paid tier of **Pie Timers specifically**.
- A permanent member number.
- Opt-in credit by first name or handle on an in-app credits screen.
- A "Foundation" badge visible in the app.

- A direct email line to you, with an honest stated response time.

### What it must not include

- Transferable or inheritable status. Tied to a person.
- Roadmap control. Input yes, votes no.
- The phrase "priority support", which implies a service level you cannot staff.

### Why Supabase, and not either store

- **Neither store can see the other.** Google Play has no idea a Paddle subscription exists, and Paddle has no idea about a Play one. If either store is authoritative, a person who bought on one rail loses access on the other.
- **You already made this decision once.** The Windows screensaver reads `identity.product_entitlements` with no fallback. Supabase is already the de facto source of truth for at least one surface. Making it so for all three is consistency, not new architecture.
- **Magic-link identity gives you the join.** A Supabase user is a person, independent of which store they happened to pay through. That is the one thing a store account cannot give you.
- **A Foundation grant becomes a row.** Insert it once, and it works on web, Android and the screensaver automatically. That is the entire payoff.
- **Rows are revocable.** Offline codes are not. This turns abuse from an unfixable problem into a fixable one.

### The architecture

1. **Identity:** the Supabase user, keyed on the magic-link email address. This is the person.
2. **Entitlement:** `identity.product_entitlements` holds capability rows per user and product. You already have `can_sync`, `can_use_calendar` and `can_use_screensaver`. Add a `foundation_member` marker alongside the same capability set the paid tier grants, plus a `source` column so you can tell a Paddle grant from a Play grant from a manual one.
3. **Writers into the table:**
   - Paddle webhook, already built and deployed.
   - **New: a Play Real-time Developer Notifications consumer.** Play publishes subscription events to a Pub/Sub topic. A Supabase edge function consumes them and writes entitlements, mirroring the Paddle webhook you already have. This is the main piece of work.
   - **New: a manual grant path** for Foundation. A row insert with `source = 'foundation'` and no expiry.
4. **Readers:** every client, Android included, reads entitlement from Supabase rather than from the store it was bought in.
5. **Failure behaviour:** fail closed on error, consistent with your screensaver decision, **but cache a valid entitlement locally for a grace window of about seven days.** Otherwise a Supabase outage locks out people who have paid, which is a worse failure than the one you were guarding against.

### The hard part: linking a Play purchase to a Supabase user

Google never gives you the purchaser's email address. You get a purchase token and, if you ask for it, an obfuscated account identifier.

- **Use `obfuscatedAccountId`.** Pass a hash of the Supabase user id when you launch the Play billing flow. When the notification arrives, you can map the purchase back to a person. This is exactly what the field exists for.
- **Consequence: the Android app must require sign-in before purchase.** That is a real friction cost on a store where people expect to just tap and buy. It is the price of a coherent cross-platform entitlement, and I think it is worth paying.
- **Fallback for a purchase made before sign-in:** keep the purchase token, and offer a "restore purchases" action on first sign-in that queries the token from the client and binds it then. Workable, messier, worth building as a safety net rather than the primary path.

### 🗂️ The roster: Play list as intake, Supabase as the record

You want the Google Play Console tester list to be the prime reference. **It is the right intake mechanism and the wrong system of record.** Use it for the first job, not the second.

**Why it cannot be the record of a lifetime promise:**

- **It is mutable with no audit history.** Anyone with Console access can add or remove an address, and nothing tells you it happened or when.
- **Accidental removal is silent and unrecoverable.** There is no undo and no log to reconstruct from.
- **It is Play-only.** It structurally cannot see a web Foundation member who came through Paddle, or the Windows screensaver. Your roster would be missing everyone who never used Android.
- **It does not outlive the platform.** If you ever stop distributing on Play, the list may simply vanish. A lifetime promise cannot depend on continuing to ship to one store.
- **It already cannot produce the roster you want.** Carole is Foundation Member #1 and almost certainly is not a Play closed tester. That is the whole argument in one example.

**What to do instead:**

1. The Play tester list runs as the **acquisition funnel**, exactly as you intended.
2. At the freeze date, **export it and snapshot it into Supabase** as the permanent, auditable roster: email, member number, source, granted date.
3. Reconcile against Supabase auth users, since a Play tester email is just an email address and magic link works to any of them. Ask each candidate to sign in on web with the same address.
4. Keep a dated CSV copy outside Supabase too. Belt and braces is proportionate for a promise with no end date.
5. After the freeze, the Play list is operational only. Changing it changes nothing about who is a Foundation member.

**Eligibility rule, written down before the freeze:** opted in to closed testing before [date] **and** submitted at least one piece of substantive feedback.

The feedback requirement matters. Google's requirement for personal developer accounts created after 13 November 2023 is 12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days, and since 2026 Google also checks genuine usage. That rule has produced a cottage industry of tester-exchange services. **If you use one to reach the 12, exclude those accounts from the roster explicitly.** They are not a community, and a locked-price promise to an account farm is cost with nothing returned.

### ⚠️ One compliance risk the dual setup creates

Google Play's Payments policy requires Play Billing for in-app purchases of digital goods, and restricts steering users to external payment. Because Foundation members **pay**, this needs more care than a free grant would. A Foundation member buying on Android must go through Play Billing at their locked price, not be routed to the Paddle checkout. Before the Android app mentions, links to, or hints at web pricing, check Play's current Payments policy for Australia. The rules have been moving through 2026 and getting this wrong risks the listing.

### ✅ This is revenue-positive from day one

The important framing, and the thing that separates this from the free version I was worried about:

- **100 members at A$19 is about A$1,900 a year of actual revenue.** Net of platform fees, roughly A$1,735 through Paddle or A$1,615 through Play.

A ten-year cost of under three thousand dollars, against nineteen thousand of revenue from the same hundred people, is not a business risk. It is a very cheap way to buy a hundred advocates, and it pays for itself many times over.

### 🔒 Making the lock durable across both platforms

This is where the promise actually breaks, and the two rails behave differently.

| Platform | Default behaviour | The risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Google Play** | Handles this natively. Change a subscription price and existing subscribers drop into a **legacy price cohort**, keeping their old price until you actively end that cohort | You end the cohort by accident, years from now, while doing a routine price change |
| **Paddle** | Does **not** grandfather implicitly. A subscription references a specific price object | Editing a live price in place can propagate to existing subscriptions on renewal, silently breaking the promise with no alert |

**The Paddle rule, in one line: never edit a live price object. Always create a new price id for a new price, and leave the Foundation price untouched and unarchived.** Name it so the next person cannot miss it, for example a description of "FOUNDATION COHORT, LOCKED, DO NOT EDIT".

**Recommended: record the promise as data in Supabase, not as a flag.**

Since `identity.product_entitlements` is already your source of truth, extend it or add a companion table holding:

- `user_id`, the Supabase user, which is the person
- `cohort`, for example `play_closed_test_2026`
- `locked_price_amount` and `locked_price_currency`
- `locked_at`, the date the promise was made
- `paddle_price_id` and `play_base_plan_id` actually in force for that person
- `granted_by` and a free-text note

Storing the **amount**, not just a boolean, is the important part. It means the promise is auditable and reconstructable even if both stores lose their cohorts, and neither platform can override it because neither platform is where the promise lives.

**Then add a reconciliation job.** A scheduled Supabase edge function, weekly, that pulls what each store is actually charging every Foundation member and compares it to `locked_price_amount`. Alert on any mismatch. This is cheap to build and it is the only control that will still be working in 2032.

### What will not work, so you do not waste time on it

- **Play promo codes.** For subscriptions these grant a **free trial, not a free subscription**, and a promotion runs for at most one year.
- **Play subscription offers.** At most two pricing phases, each with a finite recurrence count. There is no infinite phase.
- **Play licence testers.** A testing facility, not a durable entitlement.

If Paddle went to 7 percent plus US$0.75, you would net about A$16.62, a loss of roughly A$0.73 per member per year. Across 100 members that is about A$73 a year. On Play, moving from 15 to 20 percent would cost about A$95 a year across the same roster. Real, but set against A$1,900 of annual revenue it is not a reason to avoid the promise.

**The pre-flight checklist, to write once and keep in the repo as PRICING.md:**

1. Does this change touch a price object that any Foundation member is on? If yes, stop and create a new price instead.
2. In Play Console, confirm the legacy price cohort is being **retained**, not ended.
3. Run the reconciliation job manually before and after the change.
4. Confirm no Foundation member's charged amount moved.

Attach that checklist to a recurring annual Todoist task so it surfaces even in years when you change nothing.

### 📣 What to say publicly, worded so it cannot be over-read

> **Foundation members: your price is locked for life.**
>
> The first 100 people to test Pie Timers become Foundation members. **You pay the launch price, and that price never rises for you.** Not for inflation, not for cost of living, not for app store fees. One hundred subscriptions, numbered, and that is the lot.
>
> - **It is a subscription, not a free account.** You pay the launch price, permanently, at that price.
> - It covers Pie Timers on any platform we support, including if you move between the web app and Google Play. Sign in with the same email and it follows you.
> - It holds for as long as your subscription stays active. If it lapses and you resubscribe, you rejoin at the current price.
> - It covers Pie Timers only, not other AibhlínnAI products.
> - If AibhlínnAI is ever sold or wound up, we will tell you rather than quietly change it.

Each line closes a specific over-reading: **that Foundation means free**, platform migration, lapse and return, product scope, and change of entity. Publish all five or the promise means whatever the reader wants it to mean.

**The first line is the one people will get wrong.** "Foundation member" sounds like a gift, and unless the word "pay" appears early and plainly, a meaningful number of people will assume they are being given the app. That misunderstanding is much harder to correct after the fact than to prevent now.

**Words to keep out of every piece of Foundation copy:** free, complimentary, gift, on the house, no charge.

**Also safe to promise absolutely, because the cost of keeping them never grows:** no ads, no tracking, no selling your data.

### The principle worth keeping

Absolutes are safe when the cost of honouring them is bounded. Privacy commitments are decisions not to do something, so they cost the same forever. A price promise is a cost curve, which is why the scope matters more than the wording. **A frozen roster is what turns "ever" from a liability into a line item.**

**Cheapest risk control available:** a short Foundation Tier terms page on aibhlinn.ai, carrying the four lines above, linked from the email that grants the entitlement. Write it before you grant the first one.

## 4. 💼 AibhlínnAI on your LinkedIn, alongside your ATO role

**Recommendation: disclose, but do not market.** List AibhlínnAI as a separate experience entry, factually, with a plain independence statement. Then run the actual marketing from aibhlinn.ai and public ND communities, not from LinkedIn. That gets you the full benefit of transparency without creating a permanent compliance surface you have to maintain on every post.

*General guidance only. Not legal or employment advice. Run the final position past your manager and ask for it to be recorded as an addendum to your existing declaration.*

### Testing your framing first

Your read was "potential conflict of interest if I do, perceived conflict of interest if I don't". That is close, but the two sides are not symmetrical, and the correction matters.

- **Listing it does not create a conflict.** The conflict exists because the business exists. You have already declared it and notified outside employment. LinkedIn is disclosure of something already managed, not a new exposure.
- **Omitting it does not create a perceived conflict either**, at least not today. What it creates is **concealment risk**: if someone discovers the venture in 2029 and sees an ATO profile that never mentioned it, the inference available to them is that you hid it. That inference is more damaging than the underlying conflict ever was.
- So the asymmetry is real but it runs on a time axis. Listing is neutral now and protective later. Omitting is neutral now and carries a tail risk you cannot close.
- APS guidance consistently favours transparency and early disclosure over silence. **Your starting instinct was right.**

**The refinement:** LinkedIn is not where the conflict gets managed. Your declaration and management plan do that. LinkedIn just needs to be publicly consistent with what you have already told your employer. The controls are about separation, not silence.

### The three options, honestly compared

| Option | Risk now | Risk later | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **List it, market elsewhere** | Low | Low | **Recommended** |
| Leave it off LinkedIn entirely and never use LinkedIn for the venture | Low | Medium, concealment inference | Defensible, and genuinely simpler. Choose this if you want zero ongoing thought |
| List it and actively market from LinkedIn | Medium | High | Avoid. Reasons below |

### The reason not to market from LinkedIn

**Your LinkedIn network is substantially your workplace.** After years in the APS, most of your connections are ATO and APS colleagues, and a good number of them will be Neurodiversity Network members.

That means a product post on LinkedIn is not "marketing to the public". It is **marketing to colleagues through a different window**, which is exactly what section 2 concludes you cannot do. The platform changes, the power dynamic does not.

This single point does more work than any profile setting.

### How to list both without implying endorsement

- **Two separate Experience entries.** Standard employment entry for the ATO. A separate entry for AibhlínnAI, "Founder", sole trader, with its own dates.
- **Do not fuse them in your headline.** Avoid anything shaped like "EL1 at the Australian Taxation Office | Founder, AibhlínnAI". That construction lets the ATO name lend weight to the venture. Keep the headline to one identity, or keep it role-neutral.
- **Add a plain independence line** to the AibhlínnAI entry: "AibhlínnAI is my own business. It is independent of my employer and is not connected with or endorsed by the Australian Taxation Office."
- **Add "views my own"** to your About section. Cheap, standard, and it does real work.
- **Keep the Network role inside the ATO entry**, not as a standalone item. See below.

### Using the ATO name in AibhlínnAI content

**Short answer: no, anywhere except your LinkedIn employment history.**

- The ATO name belongs in your employment record, because that is what an employment record is for.
- It does not belong on aibhlinn.ai, in the app, in store listings, in a pitch, in a speaker bio, or in any post about the product.
- **The test:** would removing the ATO name make the claim weaker? If yes, the name is doing persuasive work, and that is trading on official status.
- The elevator pitches in section 1 deliberately contain no reference to your employer. Keep it that way.

### What not to post

- Any product content that leans on your ATO or Network identity.
- Product marketing generally, for the network-composition reason above.
- Comment on tax policy, ATO administration, or government policy.
- Anything that could be read as official ATO comment.
- **Likes and reshares of political or government-critical content.** APSC guidance treats liking and sharing as endorsement, as though you had written the material yourself. This catches more people than original posts do.
- Testimonials or endorsements from colleagues.

### Commenting on neurodiversity in a professional capacity

This is the genuinely difficult part, and I do not want to pretend it resolves cleanly.

- You have a real professional standing here. You are AuDHD, you co-chair the Network, and workplace neurodiversity inclusion is adjacent to your actual job. Speaking on it is part of who you legitimately are professionally.
- **But once AibhlínnAI is on your profile, every neurodiversity post carries a commercial shadow.** A post about time blindness, written by someone who sells a time blindness product, reads as content marketing whether or not you meant it that way.
- **The workable line:** speak from lived experience and from your Network role, provided the post does not mention, link to, or allude to the product, and provided you are not building an audience through the Network role that you later sell to.
- **The honest risk:** this is hard to sustain over years. If you become known for both, the separation erodes by itself, without any single post being the problem.
- The APSC risk factors are a useful self-check before posting: how senior you are, how close the content sits to your area of work, and whether the tone sits inside normal professional norms. Neurodiversity content scores **high on proximity** for you specifically, because of the Network role. That means a higher bar, not a prohibition.

### Should the Network Co-chair title appear alongside the venture?

**No. This is the clearest call in this section.**

- Listing the role on LinkedIn is fine. It is a real role and you are entitled to have it on your profile.
- But keep it **inside the ATO experience entry**, where it plainly reads as an internal staff network role, rather than as a standalone entry that could be mistaken for an external advocacy credential.
- **Never** use it in an AibhlínnAI bio, speaker bio, pitch deck, media mention or product page. "Co-chair of the ATO Neurodiversity Network" in a commercial context is the single most direct way to trade your official status for commercial advantage, and it would undo the credit you have earned by declaring properly.
- If a conference or podcast introduces you and asks for a bio, give them an AibhlínnAI-only bio and ask them not to add your employer.
