## ⚠️ Two practical things before you send anything

**Have the opt-in link ready.** Google Play closed testing is not discoverable. If someone searches the Play Store for Pie Timers they will find nothing and quietly give up. They need your specific opt-in URL, and they need to use the same Google account on their phone.

**Do not put the 14 day commitment in the first message.** Google needs testers opted in continuously for 14 days and actually using the app. That is a real ask, but it makes the opening message heavier than it needs to be. Put it in the follow-up when you send the link, so the first message stays a light yes or no.

## 🎽 On mentioning Pie Timers: honest advice first

**The t-shirt already does the work.** You will be standing at a microphone in a branded tee in front of several hundred people who are exactly your market. That is the plug. It has already landed before you open your mouth.

So the question itself should be a **real question**. This audience has an unusually fine radar for being sold to, because they get sold to constantly, by supplement companies, coaching programmes and apps. A question that is obviously a plug burns credibility with the precise people you need. A good question asked well earns it.

**The structure that works:** one line of self-identification, then a genuine question.

### 🎙️ Your line

**As you wrote it:**

> "I'm Mal Oakley, and I am a proud neurodivergent person. I have recently built a time-visibility tool for ND people."

**A tightened version for saying out loud:**

> "I'm Mal Oakley, and I'm a proud neurodivergent person. I've built a tool that makes time visible for ND people."

Both work. Pick whichever sounds like you when you say it aloud, because that is the only test that matters here.

**Two things I changed in the tightened one, and why. Take either, both, or neither.**

- **"I have recently built" to "I've built".** Spoken, the full form is stiff, and "recently" quietly works against you. It makes the tool sound untested and invites the room to discount it. You do not owe anyone a timeline.
- **"time-visibility tool" to "a tool that makes time visible".** A compound noun takes a beat to parse aloud, and a beat is a long time at a microphone. The plainer phrasing lands immediately, which suits this audience better anyway.

**What I would not touch.** "Proud neurodivergent person" is yours and it is the right call. It is warmer than a diagnosis label, it is broader so it is the safer disclosure, and it puts you in the room as one of them rather than as a specialist. Using "ND people" in the second sentence is also good instinct: you have just said "neurodivergent", and saying it twice in two sentences would drag. At this conference the shorthand needs no explaining.

**At a microphone, shorter is better.** The line is only the ticket that lets you ask the question. The question is the point. Every extra word spends attention you want the question to have.

That is disclosure, which conference etiquette expects and welcomes. It is not a pitch. Nobody will object, and the people who care will find you in the break.

**Three rules:**

1. **Use the self-identification line once, twice at the absolute most, across the whole day.** Say it at every session and you become the bloke who is selling something. That reputation is very hard to shed in a room this size.
2. **Never describe what the app does from the floor.** Naming it as a tool that makes time visible is disclosure. Anything more is an ad.
3. **Every question below is written to be worth asking even if you had nothing to sell.** If one of them stops being that, drop it.

### 🍽️ The break strategy, which is better than the microphone anyway

The 50 minute main break at 12:30 is your real opportunity. One to one, t-shirt visible, no audience, and the speaker is not performing.

Approaching a speaker in a break with a specific, informed follow-up to their talk is normal, welcome, and how every useful professional connection at these events actually starts. It is also where you can properly say what you have built without it being a performance.

**Priority order for break conversations:** Dr Siobhan Lamb first, Michelle Lewis second, Kate Donohue third. Reasoning under each speaker below.

## ⚠️ One risk to keep in mind

**If ATO colleagues or Neurodiversity Network members are in that room, the power dynamic from section 2 applies there too.** A conference hall is not a neutral space just because it is a Saturday and you are not at work.

- Being there as a private individual is entirely legitimate. This is a public ND community event, which section 2 lists as a proper channel.
- **Do not work Network contacts.** If you spot Network members, you are a fellow attendee, not the Co-chair, and definitely not recruiting.
- If someone from the Network approaches you about the t-shirt, answer honestly and briefly, and leave it there. Do not convert it into a tester ask.
- Your self-identification line should say what you build. It should **never** mention the ATO or the Network.

Also, lightly: you are an attendee, not a paid exhibitor. There are eight exhibitors who have paid for that privilege. Recruiting energetically around their tables would be poor form. Keep it to conversations that come to you.

## ❓ Questions by speaker

### 10:05 Dr Siobhan Lamb | Supporting Neurodivergent Young People and Their Families to Thrive

*Neurodevelopmental Educator, 20+ years teaching including Head of Science and Executive Functioning, accredited ADHD coach, built an executive functioning programme. Her three children and she are all neurodivergent. Special interest in twice-exceptionality.*

- **"Twice-exceptional kids often get read as either gifted or struggling, rarely both. What is the first thing a parent should push for when a school has settled on one of those stories and stopped looking for the other?"**
- "You have built an executive functioning programme. What is the most common thing families try first that does not work, and what should they be doing instead?"
- "When a child can do a thing brilliantly one day and not at all the next, how do you help a school understand that without it being read as a behaviour problem?"

**🟢 Natural opening for you: strongest of the day.** Executive function and time is exactly your domain. Her session is also directly relevant to your own two kids, so you have a real reason to talk to her in the break that has nothing to do with selling anything.

### 11:05 Kate Donohue | ADHD and Multi-Neurodivergence

*Director of Neurodivergent Education. ND-affirming training. Lived experience as a ND person and parent. Co-led intersectional autism research.*

- **"What separates a genuinely neurodivergent-affirming tool or strategy from one that is really about compliance with a dressing of affirming language? What should people look for?"**
- "For AuDHD people specifically, ADHD strategies and autism strategies often pull in opposite directions. How do you help someone work out which need to serve when they conflict?"
- "What is the most common mistake well-meaning professionals make when they have learned about autism and ADHD separately but not together?"

**🟢 Natural opening: good, and the first question is the honest one.** You build a tool for this audience, and asking what separates affirming design from compliance dressed up is a question you have a genuine stake in. Disclosing that you build something makes the question better, not worse.

### 11:50 Sandy Booysen | Gut Health, Diet and Micronutrients in Neurodivergence

*Accredited Practising Dietitian and Feeding Therapist, 15 years. ARFID, gut-brain health, food sensitivities, sensory processing.*

- **"There is a lot of confident marketing around supplements and elimination diets aimed at ND families. How should a parent tell a reasonable evidence-based suggestion from something being sold to them?"**
- "For an adult whose eating falls apart in the evening when executive function has run out, where would you start?"
- "How do you tell the difference between ARFID and sensory-driven fussiness that a child will grow through, and does the distinction change what you do?"

**🔴 No natural opening. Do not force one.** Ask a straight question. You are AuDHD with two ND kids and you have skin in this conversation without needing to mention the app.

### 13:20 Michelle Lewis | ADHD Self-Awareness: Practical Strategies at School and Home

*30+ years teaching, founder of i.am.me, late ADHD diagnosis, specialist teacher in Music and Digital Technologies in Adelaide, mother of two ADHD children.*

- **"You teach digital technologies. Most classroom tech aimed at ADHD kids is really surveillance with a friendly interface. What does a tool need to do to build a child's self-awareness rather than just manage their behaviour?"**
- "What is the single strategy you would want every South Australian classroom teacher to know, if they only ever learned one?"
- "How do you help a child build self-awareness about their ADHD without it tipping into self-criticism?"

**🟢 Natural opening: strong, and the first question is the best one of the day for you.** It is a real and slightly pointed question about tool design that she is uniquely placed to answer, and your disclosure line makes clear why you are asking. She is Adelaide-based too, so a break conversation can continue afterwards.

### 14:05 Sarah Hoff | Practical Breath and Body Tools for Everyday Stress

*ICF Professional Certified Coach, ADHD coach and counsellor, founder of The ADHD Revolution, diagnosed in midlife. Sound healing, meditation, breathwork.*

- **"Breathwork and stillness practices ask people to sit with internal discomfort, which is exactly what a lot of ADHD brains are wired to escape. How do you make these accessible to someone who finds stillness genuinely aversive?"**
- "For someone whose energy and capacity swing unpredictably day to day, how do you build a practice that does not depend on consistency to be worth doing?"
- "What would you say to someone who has tried meditation repeatedly, failed, and concluded it is not for people like them?"

**🟡 Weak opening. Skip the disclosure line here.** The second question is a genuinely useful one for you personally, given fluctuating energy, and it is better asked as yourself than as a builder.

### 15:10 Dr Aleem Khan | ADHD Unmasked: From First Signs to Lifelong Care

*Consultant Psychiatrist, FRANZCP, Elyséa HEALTH Adelaide. ADHD across the lifespan. More than 45,000 telehealth consultations delivered through his organisations.*

- **"You have delivered assessment and care at very large scale through telehealth. What has that told you about who was previously missing out, and is the bottleneck now diagnosis, prescribing, or ongoing care?"**
- "For adults diagnosed late, what does good care look like beyond medication, and what do you see people not being offered that they should be?"
- "What should a South Australian adult who suspects ADHD actually do first, given current wait times and costs?"

**🔴 No opening, and do not look for one.** A psychiatrist's Q&A is the single worst place in the building to mention a commercial product. Ask a strong clinical question and leave it entirely alone. The first one is excellent and will land well with the room.
